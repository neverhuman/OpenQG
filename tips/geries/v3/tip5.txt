## Where the archive is strong

The archive is strong on **benchmark strategy**. It understands that the immediate win is not broad fake coverage, but clean audited refusal.

It is also strong on **structural Standard Model logic**. The repeated use of the finite algebra

[
\mathcal A_F = \mathbb C \oplus \mathbb H \oplus M_3(\mathbb C)
]

is a plausible route to the Standard Model gauge package. The (M_3(\mathbb C)) factor naturally tracks color, (\mathbb H) tracks weak (SU(2)), and unimodularity/central quotient logic gives the right kind of hypercharge structure.

It is strongest on **three exact/null row families**:

[
\texttt{ligogr_*}: 0,\qquad
\texttt{microscope_*}: 0,\qquad
\texttt{sme_*}: 0.
]

These are legitimate only when the fixture rows are actually deviation/coefficient rows. MICROSCOPE, for example, is a clean WEP test; its final result reports no violation and constrains the Ti/Pt Eötvös parameter at the (10^{-15}) scale. ([arXiv][3]) SME rows also make sense only if the fixture is mapping Lorentz/CPT-violating coefficients; the SME data-table literature is explicitly about coefficients controlling Lorentz and CPT violation. ([arXiv][4])

The archive also correctly identifies that GWOSC masses, EHT angular diameters, JPL orbital elements, SPARC rotation speeds, and Pantheon distances are not pure universal-law outputs. They require historical state data. For instance, EHT’s M87* ring diameter is a measured source-specific quantity around (42\pm3,\mu\mathrm{as}), not something a law theory can emit without mass, distance, spin, inclination, and emission-state inputs. ([Illinois Experts][5]) JPL Horizons ephemerides start from state/initial-condition data such as osculating elements and then numerically integrate; they are not raw universal constants. ([JPL Solar System Dynamics][6]) SPARC likewise contains galaxy-specific photometry and rotation curves for 175 disk galaxies, so row-wise velocities require baryonic profiles and halo/history assumptions. ([ADS][7])

## Where the archive is weak

The central weakness is **score inflation**.

Many tips say, in effect: “I wrote equations for every domain and refused unsupported rows, so I score 86–88.” That is too generous. The brief does not merely ask for equations somewhere nearby. It asks for derivations, parameter origins, dimensional checks, limiting cases, source provenance, fixture mappings, and uncertainty propagation sufficient for an auditor to reconstruct every emitted number.

The missing pieces are not minor:

[
\alpha,\quad g_1,g_2,g_3,\quad v,\quad m_H,\quad Y_f,\quad
V_{\rm CKM},\quad U_{\rm PMNS},
]

[

\rho_\Lambda,\quad \eta_B,\quad \Omega_{\rm DM},\quad
A_s,\quad n_s,\quad H_0,\quad \Omega_i.
]

Without those, the candidate cannot honestly predict most CODATA, PDG, HEPData, NuFIT, Planck, DESI, BOSS, Pantheon, NIST ASD, or SPARC rows.

The archive is also weak on **implementation evidence**. No tip supplies a manifest, adapter, prediction JSONL, or actual `rtk just universal-micro` / `rtk just universal-mini` run. Most tips are dossier plans, not runnable artifacts.

The third weakness is **invented selector language**. Phrases like “spectral entropy selector,” “trace-anomaly bootstrap,” “finite torsion vacuum,” “generation index,” “flux discretuum,” or “modular determinant” are promising research ideas, but they are not derivations until they uniquely produce numbers without target data.

## What is missing

The archive needs six concrete upgrades.

First, it needs a **real dimensionless-constants derivation**, especially (\alpha), (g_i), (v/M_{\rm Pl}), Yukawas, CKM, PMNS, and (\Lambda_{\rm QCD}). Listing CODATA is not a derivation.

Second, it needs a **finite Dirac/flavor solution**. The finite spectral operator (D_F) cannot remain a container for arbitrary Yukawa matrices if the theory wants to predict particle and neutrino rows.

Third, it needs a **cosmology closure**: vacuum energy, dark matter abundance, baryogenesis, primordial perturbations, and background parameters must come from theory, not Planck/DESI/Pantheon values.

Fourth, it needs a **state ledger** with independent state packs. Object-specific rows should be predicted only when the source pack supplies non-target initial/boundary data.

Fifth, it needs **row-shape guards**. The adapter must distinguish coefficient values from upper bounds, SI anchors from dimensionless constants, event records from law observables, and normalized bins from physically identified transitions.

Sixth, it needs an actual **adapter and run evidence**.
