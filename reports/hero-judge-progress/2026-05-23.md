## Analysis

The 25-trial run produced a useful next dataset because it includes both accepted and rejected frontiers rather than only high-scoring examples. Seventeen trials promoted and eight did not, which gives enough negative rows to inspect gate behavior without treating every live generation as a success.

Promotion was driven mainly by candidate strength and rubric structure. Promoted trials averaged `0.726` overall quality and `0.874` hero lane score, while non-promoted trials averaged `0.605` overall quality and `0.582` hero lane score. Judge lane means were closer together (`0.719` promoted vs. `0.692` non-promoted), so the run suggests the current gate separates hero-candidate quality more sharply than judge patch quality.

The strongest observed correlations with overall quality were promotion score (`0.732`), hero lane mean (`0.590`), rubric quality (`0.588`), red-team resilience (`0.528`), and verifier confidence (`0.500`). Evidence grounding was positive but weaker (`0.379`). Timeout count had essentially no linear relationship to overall quality (`-0.024`), which is the intended behavior for the storage-safe substitute path: provider latency should reduce an individual lane's evidence quality when needed, but should not poison or fail the whole series.

The top overall trials were 008 (`0.905`), 017 (`0.862`), 013 (`0.844`), 014 (`0.787`), and 021 (`0.758`). The bottom trials were 023 (`0.495`), 004 (`0.503`), 007 (`0.540`), 001 (`0.583`), and 022 (`0.613`). Trial 001 is notable because it promoted despite low red-team resilience (`0.200`), which makes it a good reviewer target for checking whether the promotion gate should require a stronger red-team floor rather than relying on weighted aggregate score.

Winner distribution was reasonably balanced: `hero_generate-g001-l01` won 16 trials and `hero_generate-g001-l02` won 9 trials. That split is useful for plotting lane diversity, but the sample is still one generation per trial; it should not be interpreted as multi-generation frontier stability.

## Insights

- Keep the new `series_summary.csv` as the plotting entry point. It removes the need to join trial directories for most trend plots and carries artifact hashes for reviewer reproducibility.
- Add a reviewer view that highlights promoted trials with low red-team resilience. These are the highest-risk rows because the promotion gate accepted them despite stronger adversarial pressure.
- Track timeout substitute counts as a separate reliability metric, not as a quality metric. This run shows timeout count did not explain overall quality, but the count is still important for provider health and cost/latency decisions.
- Consider a hard or soft red-team floor for future live runs. A weighted aggregate lets a strong hero or verifier score compensate for weak red-team resilience, which may be acceptable for discovery but risky for claims intended to survive review.
- Keep JNoccio as the rotation boundary. The receipt evidence confirms ZYAL and Jekko only saw `jnoccio` and `jnoccio-fusion`, while downstream provider/model diversity stayed inside JNoccio.
