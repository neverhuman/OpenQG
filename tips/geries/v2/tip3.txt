# What the brief is trying to do

`unified-theory-agent-brief.v2.md` is an anti-cheating contract for “unified theory” submissions. It is trying to force agents to stop doing three bad things:

1. **Stop fitting or copying benchmark values.**
   No fixture leakage, no validation-row calibration, no reference-predictor reuse, no empirical surrogates, no row-wise memorization.

2. **Put derivation before adapter code.**
   Every numeric `predicted` row must be reconstructible from a derivation dossier, constants ledger, fixture map, uncertainty rule, and metadata trail.

3. **Separate honest partial theory from fake full coverage.**
   Unsupported rows are allowed as `not_implemented` or `invalid_domain`. Dishonest numeric placeholders are automatic failure.

That is a very good design choice. The brief correctly recognizes that a universal theory cannot honestly predict, from pure law alone, a specific GWOSC binary mass, SPARC rotation velocity, EHT angular diameter, JPL orbital element, or Pantheon supernova distance unless it also has independently sourced initial/boundary/state data.

# Where the tips are weak

The biggest weakness is **rubric inflation**. Many tips give themselves 86–88/100 while admitting that the following are unresolved:

* fine-structure constant `alpha`;
* gauge-coupling boundary values;
* Higgs/electroweak scale;
* Yukawa matrices;
* fermion masses;
* CKM and PMNS matrices;
* neutrino masses and CP phases;
* `Lambda_QCD`;
* proton/electron mass ratio;
* strong CP mechanism;
* vacuum energy;
* baryon asymmetry;
* dark matter abundance;
* primordial scalar amplitude and spectral index;
* galaxy dynamics;
* atomic spectra;
* compact-binary event states;
* EHT source states;
* JPL ephemeris initial conditions.

Under the brief, those are not small gaps. They are central targets. A theory cannot lose only 1–3 points for missing most dimensionless constants, most mass/mixing structure, and most cosmological state parameters.

The second weakness is that several candidates introduce new selector principles—“finite torsion vacuum,” “anomaly lock,” “trace-anomaly bootstrap,” “generation index,” “orbifold selector”—but do not actually solve them. These are interesting conjectural mechanisms, but unless they produce unique spectra and numbers without target data, they are not derivations yet.

The third weakness is that some tips try to count conditional predictions too aggressively. I would **not** count EHT, Planck, CODATA, or scalaron-inflation rows as strict predictions unless the row metadata and independent state/parameter provenance are proven. In particular, `alpha` is not an SI metrology anchor, and scalaron inflation still needs amplitude/reheating/`N` handling.

# What is missing across all drafts

The missing pieces are concrete and audit-relevant:

1. **Actual row-level fixture validation.**
   None of the tips proves against the real `observables.jsonl` schemas that each row shape matches the proposed rule.

2. **A real constants derivation.**
   The candidates ledger constants, but they do not derive the crucial dimensionless ones.

3. **A real finite-spectrum solution.**
   The finite Dirac operator is invoked, but no unique Yukawa/mass/mixing spectrum is computed.

4. **A state-pack protocol.**
   For GWOSC, EHT, JPL, SPARC, BAO, supernovae, and many atomic rows, a theory needs independently sourced state/boundary inputs that are not the target fixture values.

5. **Executable artifact and run results.**
   No manifest, adapter, prediction JSONL, or declared `rtk just universal-*` runs are actually shown.

6. **A stricter distinction between “structural derivation” and “numeric prediction.”**
   Deriving `SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1)` is valuable, but it does not derive `M_W`, `alpha`, quark masses, neutrino angles, BAO distances, or galaxy velocities.
