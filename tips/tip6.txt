Below is a best-effort open-source survey as of **May 11, 2026**. I weighted evidence in this order: reproducible open data and likelihoods, peer-reviewed or arXiv technical literature, official experiment/software repositories, then lower-weight community discussion. I would not treat X posts as evidence unless they point to a paper, dataset, or reproducible code.

## Bottom line

The strongest contenders are not one theory but a hierarchy:

1. **Best empirical baseline:** Standard Model + General Relativity + ΛCDM as effective field theories. It wins today’s data, but it is not a UV-complete unification.
2. **Best mathematical UV-unification candidate:** String/M-theory plus holography and swampland constraints. It has the richest consistency structure, but too many vacua and too few unique low-energy predictions.
3. **Best conservative quantum-gravity completion:** Asymptotic safety. It preserves QFT logic and may need only a finite number of physical parameters, but the fixed point and observables must be nailed down beyond truncations.
4. **Best geometric unification of the Standard Model and gravity:** Noncommutative geometry / spectral action. It geometrizes the SM beautifully, but Lorentzian dynamics, quantization, and exact phenomenology remain open.
5. **Best background-independent quantum spacetime programs:** Loop quantum gravity / spin foams / group field theory, causal dynamical triangulations, and causal set theory. They are serious quantum-spacetime programs, but matter unification and precision predictions are weaker.
6. **Most testable but least unified:** Horndeski / scalar-tensor / modified-gravity / MOND-like models. They are excellent stress tests for GR and ΛCDM, but most are not full unifications and many become gray-box-like if arbitrary functions are allowed.

My proposed “best-to-test” unified model is a **Spectral–Asymptotic Quantum Geometry** model: noncommutative spectral geometry supplies the SM+gravity field content; asymptotic safety supplies the UV quantum completion; a causal Lorentzian path-integral/FRG definition supplies dynamics; the low-energy limit is GR + SM + right-handed neutrinos + a physically named axion/dark-sector candidate + higher-curvature Wilson coefficients. It is not parameter-free, but every parameter is a named physical coupling, mass, mixing angle, Wilson coefficient, or initial condition.

## 2. Proposed unified theory: Spectral–Asymptotic Quantum Geometry

I will name the proposal **SAQG: Spectral–Asymptotic Quantum Geometry**.

It is deliberately conservative at accessible energies. The principle is:

> The observed world is the infrared limit of a Lorentzian spectral geometry whose inner fluctuations generate the Standard Model fields, while the quantum continuum limit is controlled by an asymptotically safe fixed point.

This is a synthesis, not an established theory. Its advantage is that it is **complete enough to implement**, uses **named physical parameters**, and reduces to the best-tested theories where those theories are already correct.

### 2.1 Fundamental kinematics

Start with a Lorentzian almost-commutative spectral geometry:

[

==========

C^\infty(M)\otimes \mathcal A_F,
\qquad

============

\mathbb C\oplus \mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C),
]

where (M) is a four-dimensional Lorentzian spin manifold and (\mathcal A_F) is the finite internal algebra that generates the SM gauge structure. The Hilbert space is

[

[

i\gamma^\mu(\nabla_\mu + A_\mu)
\otimes 1

\gamma_5\otimes D_F(H,Y,M_N)

A + JAJ^{-1}.
]

Here:

* (A_\mu) contains (U(1)_Y), (SU(2)_L), and (SU(3)_c) gauge fields.
* (H) is the Higgs doublet.
* (Y_u,Y_d,Y_e,Y_\nu) are Yukawa matrices.
* (M_N) is the Majorana mass matrix for right-handed neutrinos.
* (J) is the real structure/charge conjugation.
* Inner fluctuations of (D) produce gauge and Higgs fields, as in the spectral-action program. The spectral-action literature already derives SM-like gauge/Higgs/gravity structures from this kind of almost-commutative geometry. ([arXiv][12])

### 2.3 Quantum completion

The quantum effective action obeys an exact RG equation of Wetterich form:

[
\partial_t\Gamma_k[\Phi]
========================

\frac12{\rm STr}
\left[
\left(\Gamma_k^{(2)}[\Phi]+R_k\right)^{-1}
\partial_tR_k
\right],
\qquad
t=\ln k.
]

Dimensionless couplings

[
\tilde g_i(k)=
\left{
G(k)k^2,,
\Lambda(k)/k^2,,
c_R(k),c_C(k),,
g_1(k),g_2(k),g_3(k),,
Y(k),\lambda_H(k),\ldots
\right}
]

must approach a finite UV fixed point:

[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde g_i(k)=\tilde g_i^\star.
]

Near the fixed point,

[
\tilde g_i(k)
=============

\tilde g_i^\star
+
\sum_I C_I,V_i^{(I)}
\left(\frac{k}{k_0}\right)^{-\theta_I}.
]

Only positive critical exponents (\theta_I>0) define relevant physical parameters. This is the asymptotic-safety predictivity condition: the theory is allowed finitely many physical input parameters, not infinitely many arbitrary EFT coefficients. Asymptotic-safety research explicitly pursues this finite-parameter UV-completion idea for gravity plus matter. ([Open Access INAF][3])

SAQG is designed to pass known strong constraints first, then make controlled deviations only where the data allow them.

* No asymptotically safe fixed point exists for this matter content.
* Spectral-action boundary conditions cannot run to observed SM masses/couplings.

* Axion/neutrino/dark-sector parameter space is excluded without a predictive replacement.
* Cosmology requires an arbitrary time-varying dark-energy function rather than a physical field or RG mechanism.

## 3. Error metric for testing all theories

Every contender should be reduced to a forward model (m_D(\theta,T)) for dataset (D), with physical parameters (\theta) and theory (T).

For Gaussian data products:

[
\chi_D^2(T,\theta)
==================

\left[d_D-m_D(\theta,T)\right]^T
C_D^{-1}
\left[d_D-m_D(\theta,T)\right].
]

For event-count data:

[
-2\ln \mathcal L_D
==================

2\sum_i
\left[
\mu_i(\theta,T)
---------------

n_i\ln \mu_i(\theta,T)
\right]
+
{\rm const}.
]

For gravitational-wave strain:

[
\chi_{\rm GW}^2
===============

4,{\rm Re}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{
\left|\tilde h_{\rm data}(f)-\tilde h_{\rm model}(f;\theta,T)\right|^2
}{
S_n(f)
}
df.
]

The global likelihood is

[
\ln \mathcal L_{\rm total}
==========================

\sum_D \ln \mathcal L_D
+
\ln \pi(\theta).
]

For model comparison, report all of:

[
\Delta\chi^2,\qquad
\Delta{\rm AIC}=2k-2\ln\hat{\mathcal L},
\qquad
\Delta{\rm BIC}=k\ln N-2\ln\hat{\mathcal L},
\qquad
\Delta\ln Z,
]

where (Z) is Bayesian evidence. The most honest score is a **domain-holdout predictive score**:

[
{\rm Score}(T)
==============

-2\sum_{D\in{\rm holdout}}
\ln p(d_D\mid d_{\rm train},T).
]

That prevents a theory from fitting CMB with arbitrary functions and then claiming success before predicting DESI, GWOSC, EHT, collider, or equivalence-principle data.

## 5. Practical testing program

The most rigorous public-data benchmark would have five tracks.

**Track A: Low-energy constants and particle sector.**
Fit (g_1,g_2,g_3), Higgs parameters, Yukawas, CKM/PMNS, neutrino masses, and electroweak precision quantities using PDG, NIST/CODATA, HEPData, and CERN Open Data. SAQG must reproduce the SM where the SM is already verified.

**Track B: Spectral/asymptotic boundary test.**
Run the measured low-energy couplings upward with RG equations. Test whether a finite-dimensional spectral/asymptotic fixed-point surface exists and whether it predicts any measured coupling rather than merely fitting it.

**Track C: Cosmology.**
Implement SAQG in CLASS/CAMB. Compare against Planck, ACT, BICEP/Keck, DESI, SDSS, DES, KiDS, Euclid, Pantheon+, and Gaia. Do not allow arbitrary (w(z)); allow only Λ, physical scalar/axion sectors, neutrino masses, and named RG/higher-curvature parameters.

**Track D: Strong gravity.**
Implement modified waveform/ringdown corrections in LALSuite/PyCBC/Bilby. Fit GWOSC and compare with EHT black-hole imaging and NANOGrav pulsar timing.

**Track E: Null tests.**
Stress-test Lorentz invariance, equivalence principle, fifth forces, photon/neutrino dispersion, and cosmic-ray constraints using Fermi, IceCube, Pierre Auger, JPL Horizons, MICROSCOPE, and published GRB/LIV datasets.

The unified model wins only if it beats the baseline on predictive score without adding unphysical flexibility:

[
{\rm Win}
\iff
\Delta\ln Z>0
\quad{\rm and}\quad
