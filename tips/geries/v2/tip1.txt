I studied the brief and all eleven candidate answers. The important conclusion is sharper than the drafts themselves: **they are mostly trying to game the brief correctly, but not dishonestly**. They have figured out that the safest path is not to invent numbers, but to build a derivation-first “theory artifact” that emits only rows with fixture-independent derivations and refuses everything else.

My best final answer is: **the strongest honest candidate is a conservative spectral/anomaly/asymptotic-safety artifact, but the above-85 scores in most tips are inflated.** It is valuable as a research artifact, not yet a submit-ready unified theory.

## What the brief is really asking for

`unified-theory-agent-brief.v2.md` is not asking for a nice essay about unification. It is asking for an auditable OpenQG theory artifact with this order of operations:

1. Derivation dossier first.
2. Constants ledger second.
3. Fixture-family extraction map third.
4. Self-score before implementation.
5. Adapter only for rows whose values are derived without fixture leakage.
6. Benchmark runs only after all of that.

The most important rule is: **unsupported rows are allowed; fake predictions are fatal.**

So the actual game is not “fill all rows.” The game is:

> Build a theory whose adapter emits exactly one row per observable, but uses `predicted` only for values derived independently of the fixture. Everything else must be `not_implemented` or `invalid_domain`.

That is why all the stronger tips converge on the same safe row families:

| Fixture family | Why it is safe                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ligogr_*`     | If the IR limit is Einstein-Hilbert GR, GR-null deviations are predicted as zero.                                                                                                      |
| `microscope_*` | If all matter couples universally to one metric, Eötvös/WEP violation is zero.                                                                                                         |
| `sme_*`        | If exact local Lorentz/CPT symmetry is imposed, SME violation coefficients are zero, assuming the row is actually a coefficient-value row and not merely an upper-bound magnitude row. |

Everything else is hard because it requires dimensionless constants, particle masses, cosmological state parameters, or historical/astrophysical initial conditions.

## Where the tips are strong

The tips are strongest in five places.

First, they correctly understand the anti-leakage requirement. Most of them explicitly refuse fixture copying, benchmark feedback, neural regressors, fitted lookup tables, and post-hoc parameter tuning. That is exactly right.

Second, they correctly separate **dossier score** from **OpenQG benchmark score**. Several tips say their 86–88 score is not an official `openqg_score`. That distinction matters.

Third, they choose a plausible structural framework: almost-commutative spectral geometry plus asymptotic safety. That is a sensible direction because it can at least explain why the Standard Model gauge structure and GR-like gravity belong in the same mathematical package.

Fourth, they usually include a constants ledger. That is crucial because the brief explicitly says that listing measured constants is not a derivation.

Fifth, they mostly make the correct refusal decisions. They do not pretend to derive PDG masses, BAO distances, SPARC velocities, EHT angular scales, JPL ephemerides, or atomic spectra without the required constants and initial conditions.

## Where the tips are weak

The main weakness is **score inflation**.

Most of the 86–88/100 scores are not credible under the strict reading of the v2 brief. The tips often write equations for a domain and then treat that as “derivation completeness,” even when the actual numeric extraction is missing.

For example, writing

[
V_{\rm CKM}=U_u^\dagger U_d
]

does not derive CKM angles. Writing the Friedmann equations does not derive Planck, DESI, BOSS, or Pantheon rows. Writing the Kerr shadow formula does not derive EHT rows unless black-hole mass, distance, spin, inclination, and emission assumptions are independently supplied. Writing the QCD beta function does not derive hadron masses or nuclear observables.

The second weakness is that many “derived” structures are actually postulated. The finite algebra

[
\mathcal A_F=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)
]

can generate the Standard Model gauge structure, but the choice of that algebra is usually assumed. Three colors follow from (M_3(\mathbb C)), but (M_3) itself is chosen. Three generations are almost always assumed or labeled as an index condition without a solved selection theorem.

The third weakness is that the invented hardening terms are not doing enough work. Terms such as “spectral torsion,” “anomaly locking,” “orbifold selection,” “trace-anomaly bootstrap,” or “exceptional rank-3 module” sound promising, but the tips generally do not solve the stationary equations that would produce (\alpha), Yukawas, CKM, PMNS, (\Lambda_{\rm QCD}), vacuum energy, dark matter abundance, or primordial parameters.

The fourth weakness is source-pack provenance. The brief wants local source-pack and registry provenance. Several tips cite general papers or web links. That is fine for a research note, but it is not enough for a reviewable OpenQG artifact unless the source-pack process accepts those sources.

The fifth weakness is that some tips blur law-level observables and state-level observables. A unified theory may give the laws of compact-binary evolution, galaxy dynamics, or solar-system motion, but it does not automatically predict the historically realized state of one binary merger, one galaxy, or one planet without initial/boundary data.
