# Registry expansion

The suppressed-growth registry should grow, but V6 must be conservative about rigor. `crates/openqg-core/src/theory/certificate.rs` currently gives `planck_mu0_geff` rigor 0.3, correctly treating it as phenomenology. It also includes nDGP and f(R), but both are growth-enhancing in their healthy linear regimes. Do not promote a relation to rigor 1.0 merely because it can fit low S8; require a closed form, GR limit, domain checks, and a bound forward-model consequence.

The best near-term mechanism-backed suppressed-growth class is dark-sector momentum exchange, often called dark scattering or elastic scattering between dark matter and dark energy. See Simpson 2010, "Scattering of dark matter and dark energy"; Pourtsidou, Skordis, and Copeland 2013 on interacting dark energy perturbations; and later constraints on dark scattering models in RSD/weak-lensing data. The key point is that momentum transfer adds an effective drag term to the dark-matter Euler equation, suppressing growth without necessarily changing the background expansion much. This is a real mechanism, not a negative `mu0` label.

Registry shape: add `dark_scattering_growth_drag` to `certificate.rs`. Inputs should be a dimensionless drag amplitude such as `A_drag`, an equation-of-state parameter `w_de`, and perhaps `Omega_de0`. The closed form should not pretend to output `G_eff/G`; it should output a friction modification or a growth-equation coefficient. That implies `growth.rs` needs a new background field, e.g. `growth_friction0`, and the ODE should become `delta'' + (2 + dlnH/dN + Gamma(a)) delta' - 1.5 Omega_m(a) mu(a) delta = 0`. GR limit: `A_drag -> 0` gives `Gamma=0`. Domain: finite `A_drag >= 0`, stable dark-energy sound speed, no early-time blow-up. Rigor can be 0.8 initially if implemented as a standard parameterized drag from literature; 1.0 only once the exact model equations are encoded.

A second candidate is interacting vacuum/dark energy with energy transfer proportional to dark-matter density, often written `Q = xi H rho_de` or `Q = xi H rho_c`. Literature includes Amendola 2000 coupled quintessence, Valiviita, Majerotto, and Maartens 2008 on instabilities, and Clemson et al. 2012 parameterized interacting dark energy. Some signs and parameter ranges suppress growth, but many variants are unstable or enhance it. Registry entry should therefore be specific: `interacting_de_q_xi_rhoc_growth` is not a single universal relation. Require a perturbation-stability domain and a named gauge/parameterization. Rigor should start below 1.0 unless the full perturbation equations are implemented.

A third useful entry is massive-neutrino growth suppression. It is not modified gravity, but it is a mechanism-backed suppressed-growth relation with a strong GR limit. The approximate small-scale suppression `Delta P/P approx -8 f_nu` and growth-rate suppression relations are well known from Lesgourgues and Pastor reviews. However, sigma8 suppression from neutrino mass is scale-dependent and needs a transfer-function or Boltzmann backend for rigor 1.0. In the current pure-Rust model, add it as `massive_neutrino_growth_suppression_approx` at rigor 0.5, with domain `sum_mnu` small, linear scales, and a warning that it is not valid for precision scoring.

A fourth class is no-slip gravity / stable Horndeski subclasses that can reduce growth through Planck-mass running while keeping gravitational-wave speed luminal. Linder 2018 "No Slip Gravity" is relevant. But the V5 code's alpha-basis fields are not connected to a growth solver, and screening/adjudication is not wired into scoring. Do not add a rigor-1 alpha relation until `growth.rs` or a Boltzmann backend computes alpha-dependent observables and `vetoes.rs::adjudicate` is mandatory. Otherwise alpha becomes another novelty faucet.

Do not add self-accelerating DGP as a suppressed-growth mechanism. `sectors/ndgp.rs` correctly notes that the self-accelerating branch has ghost problems; adding it would undermine the veto-first rubric. Do not add f(R) as suppressed growth; `sectors/fr.rs` correctly gives enhanced matter coupling.

File-level policy: in `certificate.rs`, every new relation needs `relation_rigor_weight`, `relation_gr_value`, a verifier function, unit tests for correct value, wrong value, domain failure, and GR limit. In `binding.rs`, every relation must either bind to a field that the forward model uses or emit `UnimplementedModification`. In `growth.rs`, avoid squeezing non-mu mechanisms into `mu0`; add the actual ODE coefficient they modify.
