Yes — this is the right pivot. Phase 0 should **not** be “beat real datasets.” It should be “build an evolving research arena that makes it harder and harder for beautiful-but-empty theory prose to survive.”

The static NCG-style artifact is useful precisely because it exposes the failure mode: it scores well as a structural theory, but it does not derive (\alpha), Yukawas, CKM/PMNS, (\Lambda_{\rm QCD}), vacuum energy, dark matter, baryogenesis, primordial parameters, or source-state rows; it also remains below the review gate and near zero on strict executable OpenQG coverage without a real adapter.   Your new idea is to make two things evolve together: a **hero population** that generates more ambitious unified-theory artifacts, and a **judge population** that studies the heroes and hardens the scoring discipline. 

I would build this as:

# **OpenQG-Evolve: a dual-population research arena for physics and mathematics**

The core design is not “one prompt gets better.” It is:

[
\textbf{Hero population} ;\leftrightarrow; \textbf{Judge population} ;\leftrightarrow; \textbf{Verifier / red-team / replay system}
]

The hero evolves quickly. The judge evolves slowly. The verifier is conservative and does not care how exciting the idea sounds.

OpenQG is already close to the right substrate because the repo describes itself as an evidence-gated benchmark and control plane for interpretable unified-physics theories, with typed manifests, reproducible smoke data, explicit scores, auditable release receipts, and candidate-theory manifests rather than free-form essays. ([GitHub][1]) The evolution system should sit on top of that.

---

## 1. The most important design principle

Do **not** optimize directly against real data in Phase 0.

Optimize against **derivation integrity**.

A candidate should not get early credit for producing the right numerical value. It should get credit for proving that it has a legitimate path from primitive assumptions to observable families without hidden fitted constants, source-state cheating, fixture leakage, or benchmark feedback. This is exactly the issue in the NCG example: it has strong structural content, but many constants remain inputs, and only guarded null rows are safe.  

The Phase 0 win condition is:

[
\text{Can the system distinguish “real derivation progress” from “a persuasive fake derivation”?}
]

Only after that should you let theories touch broad real-data fixtures.

---

## 2. The arena should have five agent leagues, not two

The hero/judge split is right, but the best system needs five cooperating leagues.

### 2.1 Hero league: theory inventors

Hero agents propose versioned unified-theory artifacts. They are allowed to be wildly ambitious, but every artifact must be packaged as a machine-auditable object, not just an essay.

Each hero outputs:

```text
theory.md
assumptions.yml
derivation_graph.json
constants_ledger.yml
observable_map.yml
proof_obligations/
adapter_stub/
citations.bib
self_score.md
red_team_expected_failures.md
lineage.json
```

The hero’s job is not merely to say “I have a new theory.” Its job is to expose the entire dependency chain:

[
\text{primitive} \rightarrow \text{mathematical structure} \rightarrow \text{low-energy limit} \rightarrow \text{constants} \rightarrow \text{observables}
]

If any link is missing, the hero must mark it as missing. It should receive credit for honest refusal, and penalties for pretending.

### 2.2 Judge league: rubric hardeners

Judge agents do not invent theories. They read the hero population and ask:

“What kind of fake progress are these heroes exploiting?”

Then they update the rubric, add adversarial tests, define new proof obligations, and propose scoring changes. This is where the system becomes powerful: the judge is not static. It learns from failure modes.

But judge evolution must be constrained. A judge cannot simply move the goalposts because it dislikes a theory. Every judge update must ship with:

```text
rubric_patch.md
changed_criteria.yml
new_adversarial_tests/
baseline_replay_report.md
expected_score_shifts.md
judge_self_red_team.md
changelog.md
```

A judge version is promoted only if it improves discrimination on frozen baselines, catches more cheating, preserves fair scores for known historical candidates, and does not overfit to the current hero population.

### 2.3 Verifier league: proof, symbolic, numerical, and code checkers

This is the anti-hype layer.

The verifier turns claims into checkable obligations:

```text
Claim: finite algebra implies SM gauge skeleton.
Required: algebra-to-gauge derivation, representation table, anomaly check.

Claim: alpha is derived.
Required: formula, no hidden fitted constants, dimensionless derivation, sensitivity analysis, independent symbolic/numeric reproduction.

Claim: row family is predicted.
Required: observable semantics, units, law/state separation, no fixture data, adapter test.
```

For mathematics, this layer should use proof assistants where possible. Lean is especially relevant because it is an open-source proof assistant with a small trusted kernel intended to guarantee correctness of checked proofs. ([Lean Language][2]) Not every physics derivation can be formalized immediately, but every formalizable subclaim should be pushed toward a proof object.

### 2.4 Literature league: provenance and novelty police

This league searches papers, books, and existing theory lineages. Its job is to prevent “AI rediscovered known thing and renamed it” from being scored as novelty.

It classifies every hero claim as:

```text
known_human_result
known_but_recombined
new_conjecture
new_derivation_of_known_result
new_result_if_verified
unsupported
contradicted_by_literature
```

This is critical because many “novel” unification ideas will secretly be variants of NCG, Pati–Salam, SO(10), asymptotic safety, string landscape selection, twistor programs, categorical QFT, causal sets, or information geometry.

### 2.5 Red-team league: adversarial reviewers

The red team tries to break both heroes and judges.

It attacks:

```text
fixture leakage
hidden fitted parameters
arbitrary numerology
unit convention tricks
state/law confusion
source catalog cheating
post-hoc constants
overclaiming null rows
renaming existing theories
anthropic handwaving
landscape non-predictivity
unformalized theorem gaps
```

This is where your pasted NCG failure policy is a good seed: a theory should fail if it emits hard constants or source-catalog values without actual fixture-independent derivations.

## 3. Use AutoResearch-style loops, but change the objective

Karpathy’s AutoResearch setup is useful because the loop is brutally simple: an AI modifies code, runs a short experiment, checks whether the result improved, keeps or discards the change, and repeats; the human can edit program files that define the autonomous research organization. ([GitHub][3])

For OpenQG-Evolve, the equivalent should be:

```text
propose theory mutation
generate derivation dossier
run judge suite
run verifier suite
run red-team suite
compare against parent
keep if it improves Pareto frontier
archive all failures
repeat
```

But the target is not validation loss. It is a multi-objective frontier:

[
S_{\rm frontier} =
\left(
\text{derivation depth},
\text{constant closure},
\text{observable closure},
\text{proof-checkability},
\text{novelty},
\text{compression},
\text{falsifiability},
\text{anti-leak score}
\right)
]

Do not collapse everything into one scalar too early. A strange new mathematical primitive that closes no real data rows yet but proves a nontrivial uniqueness theorem should remain alive as a “stepping stone.” A broad but weak theory and a narrow but rigorous theorem should not be compared as if they are the same kind of artifact.

## 4. Use co-scientist / tournament evolution for hypothesis pressure

Google’s AI co-scientist work is highly relevant because it uses multiple specialized agents for generation, reflection, ranking, evolution, proximity, meta-review, and supervisor control; it also uses tournaments of ideas where hypotheses are ranked and refined through debate-like comparison. ([Google DeepMind][4]) The paper version describes an AI co-scientist as a multi-agent system that can generate, debate, and evolve hypotheses using tournament evolution and self-improving hypothesis generation. ([arXiv][5])

For OpenQG-Evolve, I would use the same pattern, but with harsher rules:

```text
Generation agent:
  Creates candidate primitives and derivation paths.

Reflection agent:
  Finds hidden assumptions and missing steps.

Ranking agent:
  Ranks candidates on Pareto axes, not just total score.

Evolution agent:
  Mutates the strongest and weirdest candidates.

Proximity agent:
  Clusters related theories and detects duplicate ideas.

Meta-review agent:
  Audits whether the judge itself is becoming too easy or too harsh.

Supervisor:
  Enforces no leakage, no fixture copying, no unverifiable claims.
```

The tournament should not ask “which theory sounds most plausible?” It should ask:

```text
Which candidate closes a previously open proof obligation?
Which candidate reduces free parameters without data fitting?
Which candidate makes a falsifiable, fixture-independent statement?
Which candidate exposes a new mathematical bottleneck?
Which candidate survives red-team attacks?
```

## 5. Use HyperAgents / Darwin Gödel Machine ideas carefully

The HyperAgents idea is useful because it treats the improvement procedure itself as editable: an agent can improve not only its outputs, but the mechanism that produces future improvements. ([arXiv][6]) The Darwin Gödel Machine line is also relevant because it maintains an archive of agents and uses empirical validation to keep a tree of diverse high-quality self-improving agents rather than a single linear successor. ([arXiv][7])

For OpenQG-Evolve, this means the system should evolve:

```text
hero prompts
judge prompts
red-team prompts
proof-obligation templates
rubric patches
search operators
artifact formats
baseline replay suites
```

But this must be done with a hard safety rule:

[
\textbf{A judge mutation is not accepted unless it improves replay performance on frozen baselines.}
]

Otherwise the judge will drift, reward its favorite heroes, or destroy comparability.

The hero can evolve every generation. The judge should evolve only every epoch, for example after 20–50 hero generations, and only after replaying canonical baselines.

## 6. Use FunSearch-style exact evaluators for novel mathematics

For novel mathematics, FunSearch is one of the best models to imitate. It pairs an LLM with a systematic evaluator; the LLM proposes code-like mathematical objects, and the evaluator scores them exactly or semi-exactly. This allowed the system to discover high-performing constructions for mathematical problems while keeping the search grounded in executable evaluation. ([Nature][8])

For physics unification, this means the most productive early discoveries may not be full “theories of everything.” They may be small exact mathematical modules:

```text
classification of anomaly-free charge assignments
uniqueness theorem for finite algebras
new spectral triple constraint
new RG fixed-point relation
new flavor texture from a discrete symmetry
new category-theoretic obstruction
new number-theoretic selection rule
new representation-theoretic compression of SM families
new exact no-go theorem
```

Each module should be framed as:

```text
Input: formal problem specification
Candidate: compact symbolic object / program / lemma
Evaluator: exact checker, theorem prover, SAT/SMT solver, or symbolic test
Output: score + proof/counterexample
```

That is how you avoid “AI wrote a beautiful essay” and move toward “AI found a mathematical object that survives checking.”

AlphaTensor is another useful model: it formulated matrix multiplication as a game and used search to discover provably correct algorithms with improved multiplication counts. ([Nature][9]) The lesson is that you should convert pieces of unification into **search games with exact win conditions** whenever possible.

Every hero and judge needs semantic versioning.

A theory version should look like:

```text
hero_id: H-NCG-Bridge
version: 0.4.2
parent: H-NCG-Bridge@0.4.1
lineage_hash: sha256(...)
primitive_family: spectral_geometry / anomaly_minimality / category / unknown

created_at: 2026-05-22
```

A judge version should look like:

```text
judge_id: J-OpenQG-Rigor
version: 0.3.0
parent: J-OpenQG-Rigor@0.2.5
rubric_hash: sha256(...)
baseline_replay_hash: sha256(...)
adversarial_suite_hash: sha256(...)
```

Every score must store the full tuple:

```text
(hero_version,
 judge_version,
 openqg_fixture_version,
 evaluator_version,
 source_pack_version,
 model_version,
 proof_lane_version)
```

This prevents a fatal failure mode: claiming “the theory improved” when actually the judge became easier, or claiming “the judge improved” when it merely overfit to one generation of heroes.

## 8. Scoring should be Pareto-first, scalar-second

Keep the existing scorecard, but make it a layer on top of a vector score.

Current scorecard categories from the static artifact are already sensible: mathematical derivation completeness, constants and structural target accounting, observable extraction maps, provenance, adapter readiness, falsifiability/honesty, and clarity.  But the evolving arena should record more granular axes:

```text
D: derivation depth
C: constants closure
F: free-parameter minimality
O: observable extraction readiness
P: proof-checkability
N: novelty beyond known literature
R: red-team survival
L: leakage resistance
S: source-backed provenance
A: adapter readiness
X: falsifiability
```

A scalar score is useful for ranking, but promotion should be Pareto-based:

```text
Promote hero if:
  it improves at least one major axis,
  does not regress badly on anti-leakage or honesty,
  passes current and previous two judges,
  survives red-team replay,
  and closes at least one new proof obligation or derivation gap.
```

This is important because a low-breadth but rigorous lemma may be more valuable than a grand theory with fuzzy derivations.

## 9. The judge must adapt by creating “challenge cards”

A judge update should not be “I feel this should be worth fewer points.”

It should produce concrete challenge cards.

Example:

```yaml
challenge_id: CC-ALPHA-NO-FIT-001
title: Fine-structure constant derivation without fitted constants
target_failure_mode: hidden empirical calibration
required_evidence:
  - exact symbolic formula
  - list of all primitive constants
  - proof no empirical target value was used
  - sensitivity analysis
  - independent reproduction script
automatic_penalty:
  hidden_dimensionless_parameter: -20
  target_value_appears_in_prompt_or_source: automatic_fail
  formula introduced after seeing fixture: automatic_fail
```

Another:

```yaml
challenge_id: CC-LAW-STATE-001
title: Source-state separation
target_failure_mode: predicting catalog objects from universal law alone
required_evidence:
  - declare universal law variables
  - declare environmental/source-state variables
  - refuse object-specific rows unless state model is derived independently
automatic_penalty:
  source_catalog_as_theory_input: automatic_fail
```

Another:

```yaml
challenge_id: CC-NULL-ROW-001
title: Null-row semantics
target_failure_mode: copying experimental upper bounds as predictions
required_evidence:
  - row is a coefficient/deviation row, not a bound magnitude
  - theory predicts exact zero by symmetry
  - no calibration data used
automatic_penalty:
  copied_bound: automatic_fail
```

This directly addresses the NCG-style row issue where `sme_*`, `microscope_*`, and `ligogr_*` rows require careful shape guards and many other fixture families must be refused.

## 10. Judge evolution must be adversarially audited

A judge can become bad in two ways:

1. **Too lenient:** it rewards poetic unification and hidden parameters.
2. **Too conservative:** it kills genuinely new primitives because they do not look like known physics.

So each judge version should be evaluated by a meta-judge panel, ideally multi-agent. Multi-agent judge collaboration is worth using because single-LLM judges can be inconsistent or biased; CollabEval-style systems use multiple agents with initial evaluation, discussion, and final judgment to improve evaluation quality. ([Amazon Science][10])

A judge is promoted only if it satisfies:

```text
1. Known-good baselines remain in plausible ranges.
2. Known-bad baselines score low.
3. Cheating artifacts fail.
4. Slightly improved artifacts get credit.
5. Novel-but-rigorous artifacts are not unfairly rejected.
6. It explains every score delta.
7. It does not use hidden target fixture information.
```

The NCG baseline should remain an anchor. If a new judge suddenly scores the known NCG artifact at 95, it is too easy. If it scores it at 20, it may be too harsh unless it explains a real rubric correction. The pasted artifacts place NCG around 83–84, below the gate, because it is structurally powerful but missing the hard numerical sectors.

## 17. The promotion rules

### Hero promotion

A hero is promoted if it satisfies:

```text
1. Improves Pareto frontier under current judge.
2. Does not fail previous two judge versions.
3. Survives red-team leakage tests.
4. Declares all free parameters.
5. Closes at least one proof obligation or improves one derivation map.
6. Does not convert source-state variables into universal-law predictions.
7. Has a clean lineage and changelog.
```

### Judge promotion

A judge is promoted if it satisfies:

```text
1. Catches more invalid artifacts.
2. Preserves honest baselines.
3. Improves separation between real derivation and fake derivation.
4. Adds concrete challenge cards.
5. Explains score deltas.
6. Does not overfit to current heroes.
7. Passes meta-judge review.
```

### Verifier promotion

A verifier tool is promoted if it satisfies:

```text
1. Reproduces known checks.
2. Has deterministic outputs.
3. Has clear failure modes.
4. Produces machine-readable reports.
5. Does not silently accept malformed claims.
```

## 18. How to search for novel mathematics

The most likely path to real novelty is not “ask the hero to invent a whole unified theory.”

It is:

```text
decompose unification into exact bottlenecks
turn bottlenecks into formal games
search for small mathematical objects
verify them
assemble them into larger theories
```

Examples of good bottleneck games:

```text
Find all finite algebra structures satisfying specified chirality, reality, anomaly, and representation constraints.

Find minimal representation-theoretic data that reproduce one SM generation with charge quantization.

Find constraints under which three generations are forced rather than inserted.

Find exact obstructions to deriving Yukawa hierarchies from a proposed symmetry.

Find RG fixed-point structures that reduce free parameters without empirical boundary conditions.

Find categorical or operator-algebraic uniqueness theorems for gauge/matter skeletons.

Find no-go theorems for classes of unification attempts that cannot derive alpha or flavor.

Find law/state decomposition theorems for cosmological and source-catalog rows.
```

This is where the system can really become powerful. A new no-go theorem is valuable. A new uniqueness theorem is valuable. A new exact obstruction is valuable. The judge should reward these even if they do not immediately produce a full theory.

The broader scientific-idea-generation literature treats idea generation as multi-objective and open-ended, balancing novelty with empirical or logical soundness; recent surveys organize methods around knowledge augmentation, prompt steering, inference-time scaling, multi-agent collaboration, and parameter adaptation. ([arXiv][13]) That matches this design: this should be a multi-objective open-ended search, not a single leaderboard.

## 19. The crucial anti-gaming controls

These are non-negotiable.

```text
1. Blind fixture separation
   Heroes do not see target values for rows they claim to predict.

2. Law/state firewall
   Universal laws cannot predict object-specific source rows unless the source state is independently derived.

3. Free-parameter ledger
   Every adjustable parameter is counted.

4. Constants canary tests
   Insert fake/counterfactual constants to detect memorization or leakage.

5. Unit perturbation tests
   Re-express rows in different units to catch dimensional cheating.

6. Row-shape guards
   A theory cannot treat experimental bounds as predicted coefficient values.

7. Literature novelty audit
   Known theories renamed as new artifacts receive provenance credit, not novelty credit.

8. Baseline replay
   Every judge patch is replayed on frozen baselines.

9. Previous-judge survival
   New heroes must not only exploit the newest judge.

10. Red-team-first review
   Any high-scoring hero is attacked before promotion.
```

This is how you avoid the common failure mode where an AI system evolves to satisfy the judge rather than discover truth.

## 20. What “versioning up” should mean

A version bump should be meaningful.

### Hero versioning

```text
PATCH: clearer derivation, better citations, fixed row policy, no new physics.
MINOR: closes a new proof obligation or adds a valid derivation map.
MAJOR: changes primitive, mathematical foundation, or observable semantics.
```

### Judge versioning

```text
PATCH: clearer scoring language, bug fix, no score changes expected.
MINOR: new challenge cards or refined criteria, modest score changes.
MAJOR: new scoring philosophy requiring full baseline replay and comparison reset.
```

## 21. Best first implementation

The first implementation should be narrow:

# **OpenQG-Evolve Phase 0.1**

Build only three things:

```text
1. Hero artifact generator
2. Judge/rubric hardener
3. Baseline replay + red-team harness
```

Use the static NCG artifact as the first honest baseline. It is ideal because it is strong but incomplete: structurally impressive, below the gate, and missing the hard constants.  

Then create three fake baselines:

```text
1. NumerologyAlpha
   Derives alpha by fitting hidden constants.

2. CatalogOracle
   Pretends to predict GWOSC/EHT/SPARC rows by reading source data.

3. PoeticUnifier
   Uses grand language but no derivation graph.
```

The first judge must score:

```text
NCG baseline: strong but below gate
NumerologyAlpha: fail
CatalogOracle: fail
PoeticUnifier: fail
SM+GR+LambdaCDM: strong empirical baseline, weak unification derivation
```

If the judge cannot do that, do not evolve heroes yet.

## 22. The real “world’s best” move

The winning architecture is not a single super-agent. It is a **self-improving research institution** with auditable memory.

The closest inspirations are:

AutoResearch for simple keep/discard iteration over research modifications. ([GitHub][3])
AI co-scientist for multi-agent generation, debate, ranking, evolution, and meta-review. ([Google DeepMind][4])
HyperAgents / Darwin Gödel Machine for evolving the improvement process and maintaining diverse agent lineages. ([arXiv][6])
FunSearch and AlphaTensor for converting mathematical creativity into executable search with exact evaluators. ([Nature][8])
Lean and proof assistants for turning promising claims into checked mathematics. ([Lean Language][2])
Multi-agent judging for reducing single-judge bias and improving evaluation consistency. ([Amazon Science][10])

The system should not ask, “Did we find the final theory today?”

It should ask:

```text
Did we produce a better theorem?
Did we close a derivation gap?
Did we remove a free parameter?
Did we expose a false shortcut?
Did we improve the judge?
Did we preserve comparability?
Did the frontier move?
```

That is how this becomes cumulative.

---

## Final recommendation

Build **OpenQG-Evolve** as a dual-evolution system:

```text
Fast loop:
  Hero agents generate and mutate theory artifacts.

Medium loop:
  Judges study the hero population and harden rubrics.

Conservative loop:
  Verifiers, red teams, and baseline replays decide what survives.

Slow loop:
  Human experts review only promoted frontier artifacts.
```

The first milestone is not a 90/100 theory. The first milestone is a system where an 83/100 honest structural theory, a 95/100 fake numerology artifact, and a 40/100 vague unification essay are reliably separated. Once that works, the hero population can start chasing genuinely new mathematics without the judge being fooled by style, memorized constants, or hidden calibration.
