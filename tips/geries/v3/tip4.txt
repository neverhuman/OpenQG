## What the tips are strong at

They correctly understand the brief’s central rule: **unsupported rows are acceptable; dishonest numeric rows are fatal.** The best tips do not copy CODATA, PDG, Planck, DESI, SPARC, NIST ASD, EHT, JPL, NuFIT, GWOSC, or benchmark outputs into “predictions.” That matters because CODATA and PDG are compilations/adjustments of measured constants and particle properties, not derivations a new theory can reuse as predictions. ([NIST][3])

They also correctly identify the three immediately safe row families: `ligogr_*`, `microscope_*`, and shape-validated `sme_*`. A metric GR infrared limit predicts zero deviation in GR null-test rows; MICROSCOPE is a WEP null-test surface; and exact Lorentz/CPT symmetry predicts vanishing SME coefficients, subject to row-shape validation. ([arXiv][4])

Their best insight is the **law/state split**. A universal law can give a Kerr-shadow formula, a Friedmann equation, a Schrödinger/QED atomic solver, or a galaxy dynamics equation. It cannot honestly predict a specific EHT angular diameter, JPL orbital element, GW event mass, Pantheon distance modulus, or SPARC rotation point without independent state data. EHT’s M87* crescent diameter, for example, is an observed source-specific quantity, not a universal constant. ([arXiv][5])

## What they are weak at

The recurring weakness is **score inflation**. Several tips award 86–88 because they write formal equations for every domain, but the brief requires derivations that can emit fixture-independent numbers. Writing the CKM formula does not derive CKM angles. Writing Friedmann equations does not derive Planck/DESI/Pantheon rows. Writing the QCD beta function does not derive hadron masses. Writing a Kerr-shadow equation does not derive an EHT row unless mass, distance, spin, inclination, and emission assumptions are independently supplied.

The speculative selector mechanisms are also under-proven. “Finite spectral torsion,” “trace-anomaly bootstrap,” “modular determinant flavor,” “spectral entropy,” “anomaly lock,” and “generation index = 3” are promising research ideas, but none of the tips actually solves the stationary equations and produces α, Yukawas, CKM, PMNS, ΛQCD, vacuum energy, dark matter abundance, baryon asymmetry, or primordial parameters without empirical input.

The archive also lacks an executed artifact. None of the tips shows a completed `manifest.yml`, adapter, one row per fixture observable, and `rtk just universal-micro` / `rtk just universal-mini` run. So any score here is a **pre-submission dossier score**, not an official OpenQG score.
