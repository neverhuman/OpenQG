## Why this human theory wins over other published candidates

**Winner: noncommutative geometry / spectral action.** The spectral action principle defines an action for a noncommutative space ((\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D)), and its original paper states that applying it to the Standard Model spectrum gives the Standard Model action coupled to Einstein plus Weyl gravity, with high-scale gauge-coupling relations. ([arXiv][1]) Later Connes–Chamseddine work frames spacetime as a product of a four-dimensional manifold and a finite noncommutative space, deriving the particle spectrum and SM symmetries “almost uniquely” under stated assumptions. ([arXiv][2]) The Chamseddine–Connes–Marcolli version includes neutrino mixing and presents an effective unified theory minimally coupled to gravity. ([arXiv][3])

**Runner-up: heterotic string standard models.** Heterotic compactifications are more ambitious as quantum gravity, and published heterotic standard-model constructions can produce realistic vacua with (SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)*Y\times U(1)*{B-L}), three families, right-handed neutrinos, and no exotic observable matter in some sectors. But the same source explicitly leaves moduli and extra structure, and the compactification/vacuum choice is not a unique fixture-independent derivation of observed constants. ([City Research Online][4]) Under this archive’s scorecard, that loses heavily.

**Asymptotic safety is not the winner.** It is a strong UV-completion program for gravity and matter via an interacting RG fixed point, but current reviews still describe open questions, and practical predictivity depends on RG trajectories with remaining free parameters fixed by experiment. ([Frontiers][5]) That is not enough for OpenQG constants, flavor, particle spectra, or cosmological-state rows.

So the best known human candidate is not “string theory in general” or “asymptotic safety in general,” but the **spectral Standard Model / spectral action** in the Connes–Chamseddine–Marcolli line.

## Known-theory-only dossier

### Foundations

Use an almost-commutative spectral triple:

[
(\mathcal A,\mathcal H,D,J,\gamma)
]

with product geometry

[
\mathcal X=M\times F,\qquad
\mathcal A=C^\infty(M)\otimes \mathcal A_F .
]

For the Standard Model finite space, the relevant internal algebra is the noncommutative finite geometry whose gauge structure yields the SM group. The open-access van Suijlekom derivation gives the unimodular gauge group as the Standard Model gauge group modulo its finite kernel:

[
\mathfrak G_{\rm SM}
====================

U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)/\mu_6 .
]

([Springer][6])

The bosonic action is the spectral action:

[
S_B=\operatorname{Tr} f(D_A/\Lambda),
]

and the fermionic action is

[
S_F=\frac12\langle J\psi,D_A\psi\rangle ,
]

with (D_A) the fluctuated Dirac operator. The original spectral-action paper gives the same basic action principle and says it yields the Standard Model action coupled to Einstein plus Weyl gravity when applied to the noncommutative SM geometry. ([arXiv][1])

### What it does **not** derive

The known theory does **not** derive the values needed for most OpenQG rows. Crucially, Chamseddine and Connes explicitly say that the number of generations remains an input in the “Why the Standard Model” construction. ([arXiv][7]) The finite Dirac operator contains Yukawa and Majorana matrices, but the observed values of fermion masses, CKM angles, PMNS angles, CP phases, and neutrino splittings are not derived fixture-independently.

The early spectral-SM Higgs-mass prediction is also not usable as a strict OpenQG prediction. A later Chamseddine–Connes paper says the singlet scalar modifies the RG analysis, invalidates the previous (160\text{–}180\ \mathrm{GeV}) Higgs prediction, and restores consistency with the lower Higgs mass. ([Springer][8]) That is a legitimate published correction, but it means I should not count Higgs mass as a clean derived row.
