# Executive critical review

V5 is a real improvement over V4.1. The important change is not that it found a better number; it is that claims now have to touch the computed background. `crates/openqg-core/src/theory/binding.rs::bind_modified_background` closes the old "certified modified gravity, computed LCDM" arbitrage for registered relations such as `ndgp_geff_over_g`, `ndgp_beta_from_omega_rc`, `fr_alpha_m`, and `planck_mu0_geff`. The novel-prediction audit in `binding.rs::audit_novel_predictions` is also a good step: witnesses are compared against `BackgroundForwardModel`, and the scorecard no longer trusts the proposer to declare distinctness.

But I would not call V5 ready for a V6 scientific claim. Go for V6 engineering, no-go for external physics claims. The run-data champion is best read as a red-team exploit that the engine discovered against its own current scoring contract. In `run-data/v5-chunk-1-white-paper.json`, the champion background is `h=0.7176575652249619`, `omega_m=0.2822055296326261`, `w0=-1.1443535961059987`, with nonzero alpha fields `alpha_m=-0.3488`, `alpha_b=-0.00913`, `alpha_k=-0.47755`, `screening="vainshtein"`, and `screening_recovery=null`. The scorecard still reports `free_dof=0`, no kill reasons, `parsimony=12`, `robustness=13`, and `novel_prediction=10` without prediction witnesses. That is not just "CMB degeneracy valley"; it is a composite scoring hole.

Top risk 1: promotability adjudication is not in the scoring path. `crates/openqg-core/src/theory/vetoes.rs::adjudicate` correctly rejects unquantified screening with `ScreeningRecoveryUnquantified`, checks the real GW170817 tensor-speed bound, and probes unphysical backgrounds. `score_candidate` calls `evaluate`, which calls `run_veto_cascade`, but neither `evaluate` nor `scorecard::score` calls `adjudicate`. The V5 champion therefore passes triage while failing the stricter check the code itself says a promotable candidate must clear.

Top risk 2: background drift is free. `Theory.parameters` still contains baseline `H0=67.4` and `Omega_m=0.315`, while `Theory.background` carries the evolved `h`, `omega_m`, and `w0`. `scorecard.rs::free_dof` counts only `Theory.parameters`, and `proposal.rs::proposal_into_theory` treats background overrides as direct fields, not provenanced parameters. Mutations in `mutation.rs::mutate` can move `background.h`, `background.omega_m`, and `background.w0`; parsimony does not charge them and derivation does not obligate them.

Top risk 3: covariance support exists but the campaign path uses diagonal likelihood. `crates/openqg-core/src/scoring/covariance.rs::score_metrics_cov` is the right abstraction, but `theory/evaluate.rs::evaluate` and `zyal_genome/physics_score.rs::baseline_log_likelihood` call `score_metrics`, not `score_metrics_cov`. Thus the "Delta lnZ about 55" headline is not covariance-aware. This is especially damaging for compressed CMB priors and same-tracer BAO pairs.

Top risk 4: novelty has a standing half-credit faucet. In `scorecard.rs`, any physically distinct theory with no prediction audits gets `nov_raw=0.5`. The alpha-jittered champion gets 10 points of novelty with no witness. That was probably meant as "distinct but undeclared"; in an evolutionary loop it is free reward for tiny non-GR metadata perturbations.

Top risk 5: the jekko loop improves yield but creates a monoculture. `proposer_memory.rs` injects deterministic DO/DON'T lines and a data brief that says growth must be suppressed with `planck_mu0_geff`. The report says 49/49 proposals converged to suppressed growth. That is observability, but not diversity.

V6 should first make the score path identical to the promotability path: `run_veto_cascade` plus `adjudicate` plus covariance-aware likelihood plus provenanced background parameters. Only after that should the team add re-clothing or registry expansion.

The go/no-go line for V6 should be operational: a replay of the six V5 chunks must either disqualify the old champions or report their real costs. If a replay still produces a 55-point candidate with nonzero alpha fields, no quantified screening, free moved background coordinates, and diagonal likelihood, then V6 has not addressed the failure. If the score drops for transparent reasons and a lower-scoring but fully receipted proposal survives, that is progress. The engine's strength is not that it avoids embarrassing outputs; it is that it can turn them into executable tests. The V5 archive gives exactly those tests.

My bottom line: V5 is a successful hardening campaign as software, and an unsuccessful campaign as physics evidence. That is not a failure; it is the correct intermediate state for an adversarial discovery engine. V6 should advertise the former and withhold the latter until the old champions are no longer cheap.
