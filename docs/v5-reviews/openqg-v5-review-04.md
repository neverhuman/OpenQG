# The degeneracy champion

The six-chunk result is partly a legitimate diagnosis of the admitted evidence set, but not cleanly. The team frames the champion as an evolved LCDM point with `h about 0.718`, `Omega_m about 0.282`, and nearly constant `Omega_m h^2`. That is visible in `run-data/v5-chunk-*.json`: chunk 1 has `h=0.7176575652249619`, `omega_m=0.2822055296326261`; chunk 2 has `h=0.7226814531417498`, `omega_m=0.2779880269602125`; chunk 6 has `h=0.7178175920461305`, `omega_m=0.28010496964718623`. Those are all close to the CMB distance-prior degeneracy valley.

However, calling it simply an LCDM degeneracy champion misses two implementation facts. First, the serialized backgrounds are not LCDM: chunks also move `w0` to approximately `-1.11` to `-1.16`, and some carry `mu0=-0.1`. Second, the champion has nonzero alpha-basis fields in at least chunk 1 (`alpha_m=-0.3488`, `alpha_k=-0.4776`) and still receives no screening-recovery kill. That means the result is not merely "compressed CMB cannot close the valley"; it is also "the engine lets unprovenanced background and alpha coordinates drift for free."

The likelihood critique is severe. `crates/openqg-core/src/scoring/covariance.rs` implements `LikelihoodData`, `CovarianceBlock`, and `score_metrics_cov`, but `crates/openqg-core/src/theory/evaluate.rs::evaluate` calls `score_metrics`, which sums independent Gaussians. `crates/openqg-bench/src/zyal_genome/physics_score.rs::baseline_log_likelihood` also calls `score_metrics`. Thus the V5 campaign path does not use covariance-aware scoring. The report's "no covariances" caveat is not a minor statistical footnote; it invalidates the magnitude of `Delta lnZ about 55` for correlated CMB/BAO/SNe-style data.

The immediate data-layer change should be explicit, not hidden behind optional blocks. Add a `LikelihoodData` loader to `crates/openqg-data/src/registry.rs` that loads records plus named covariance blocks from manifests. Extend `DatasetManifest` in `crates/openqg-core/src/validation/manifest/dataset.rs` with fields like `likelihood_kind: "diagonal" | "block_covariance" | "full_covariance"`, `covariance_path`, `observable_order`, and `normalization_included`. Then change `evaluate` and `baseline_log_likelihood` to accept `LikelihoodData` rather than `&[ObservableRecord]`. Diagonal data should be represented as a `LikelihoodData::diagonal`, not a separate scoring path.

The first required block is the Planck compressed distance prior as a 3x3 covariance over `(cmb_R, cmb_lA, cmb_omega_b_h2)`. The forward model already predicts all three in `cosmology/forward.rs`. Use a published Planck 2018 distance-prior covariance, not independent sigmas. Expected outcome: the h/Omega_m valley should narrow sharply because `R`, `l_A`, and `omega_b h^2` are not independent pulls, and their covariance encodes the direction that Planck actually allows. The champion may still improve local H0 under the compressed prior, but `Delta lnZ` should drop substantially and likely stop saturating the 20-point data-fit component.

The second required block is BAO covariance. Same-tracer `D_M/r_d` and `D_H/r_d` pairs at one redshift are correlated, and isotropic/eBOSS/DESI aggregates should not be double counted. Represent each survey-release tracer as one covariance block: e.g. `DESI_DR1_BGS_z0.30`, `LRG_z0.51`, `LRG_z0.71`, `ELG_z0.93`, `QSO_z1.49`, with ordered IDs matching the published matrix. If only `D_V/r_d` is admitted, it is a scalar block.

The third required addition is SNe with a covariance matrix, or do not count SNe yet. A diagonal list of distance moduli would be a fake precision amplifier. For Pantheon+/SH0ES-style use, define `mu@z` observables and a dense covariance matrix; if the Rust path cannot handle thousands of points efficiently, start with compressed nuisance-marginalized bins and record the loss.

Finally, the data-fit mapping in `scorecard.rs::data_fit_raw` saturates too fast: scale 5 nats means `Delta lnZ=55` and `Delta lnZ=20` are both essentially full credit. That is acceptable for a coarse rubric, but bad for diagnosing regressions. V6 should store raw `Delta lnZ`, covariance mode, number of effective data modes, and parameter count in the champion report, and gate "scientific champion" on unsaturated evidence thresholds outside the 100-point score.

One more expected outcome should be stated before the run: a covariance-aware replay may still prefer a shifted background over Planck LCDM on the admitted late-time data. That would not validate the champion as a theory; it would validate the evidence-set diagnosis. A legitimate result would say, for example, "with Planck distance-prior covariance, BAO blocks, and SNe covariance, the best unstructured background shift has Delta lnZ X but pays Y background degrees of freedom and earns zero novelty." That is a much more useful scientific statement than a saturated 55-point score.
