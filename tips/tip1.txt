## Bottom line

The **model to beat** is still **Standard Model + general relativity + ΛCDM + neutrino masses**, even though it is not a true unified theory. The strongest current unification programs are mostly **mathematical UV-completion programs** rather than experimentally confirmed alternatives. Quantum-gravity phenomenology reviews still emphasize that no clear quantum-gravity signal has been established, and that most approaches must pass through effective, low-energy observables before they can be tested. ([arXiv][1])

My best proposal below is therefore deliberately conservative: a **named-parameter, non-gray-box unified effective theory** that reduces exactly to the best-tested physics at accessible energies, but adds a small number of physically interpretable handles where public data can actually discriminate: early-universe curvature, neutrino mass, axion dark matter, and possibly slowly rolling dark energy.

## Current empirical situation

Several recent public-data streams are especially important:

DESI and related large-scale-structure data are now among the strongest tests of cosmic expansion and growth. Recent DESI analyses using millions of galaxies generally find gravity consistent with GR in modified-gravity parameterizations, while DESI DR2 BAO plus CMB/SNe combinations have produced a statistically interesting but not yet definitive preference for evolving dark energy. ([News][13])

Strong-field gravity is also becoming sharper. LVK open gravitational-wave data, black-hole ringdowns, EHT images of M87*/Sgr A*, and pulsar timing arrays all constrain deviations from GR, but none has yet forced a replacement of GR. ([MPG][14])

Particle physics remains brutally constraining. CERN Open Data provides multi-experiment collider data, HEPData stores publication-level high-energy-physics tables, PDG aggregates particle properties and BSM limits, and precision sources such as CODATA/NIST and NuFIT tightly constrain any theory that changes constants, particle spectra, or neutrino physics. ([CERN Open Data][15])

# My proposed unified model: **MSA-U**

I will call the model **MSA-U: Minimal Spectral-Asymptotic Unification**.

It is not a “black box.” Every parameter has a physical name, dimension, and measurement target. It is designed to be falsifiable against public data and to nest the current best model as a limiting case.

### Core idea

MSA-U combines four principles:

1. **Low-energy limit:** reproduce GR + SM + massive neutrinos + cold dark matter + dark energy.
2. **Geometric unification:** use spectral-action/noncommutative-geometry logic to treat gauge/Higgs/gravity as geometric data.
3. **Quantum completion:** impose an asymptotic-safety UV condition so only finitely many relevant couplings remain free.
4. **Observable minimality:** add only physically named sectors that data already demand or strongly motivate: neutrino masses, dark matter, early-universe curvature, and possibly dynamical dark energy.

This is a candidate theory, not an established truth. Its best feature is that it is directly testable: if data prefer pure ΛCDM+GR, the extra parameters run to their decoupling values.

## 1. Fields and symmetries

Use natural units (c=\hbar=1). The infrared spacetime is a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold (M) with metric (g_{\mu\nu}), tetrad (e^a_\mu), and spin connection.

Gauge group:

[
G_{\rm SM}=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y .
]

Matter fields per generation:

[
q_L,;u_R,;d_R,;\ell_L,;e_R,;\nu_R .
]

The right-handed neutrinos (\nu_R) allow a seesaw mechanism for neutrino masses.

Bosonic fields:

[
g_{\mu\nu},\quad A^a_\mu,\quad W^i_\mu,\quad B_\mu,\quad H,\quad a,\quad \phi .
]

Here:

* (H) is the Higgs doublet.
* (a) is the QCD axion/dark-matter field.
* (\phi) is a minimally coupled dark-energy scalar. If its slope is zero, it becomes an ordinary cosmological constant.

## 3. Quantum definition and UV completion

The quantum theory is defined Wilsonianly by an effective average action (\Gamma_k):

[
Z[J]
====

\int
\frac{
\mathcal D g,\mathcal D A,\mathcal D\psi,\mathcal D H,\mathcal D a,\mathcal D\phi
}{
{\rm Diff}(M)\times G_{\rm SM}
}
\exp\left[
iS_{\rm MSA-U}
+i\int J\Phi
\right].
]

The running action obeys the Wetterich-type functional RG equation:

[
k\partial_k\Gamma_k
===================

\frac12
{\rm STr}
\left[
\left(\Gamma_k^{(2)}+\mathcal R_k\right)^{-1}
k\partial_k\mathcal R_k
\right].
]

The asymptotic-safety condition is

[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde g_i(k)=\tilde g_i^\star,
]

with only finitely many relevant directions:

[
N_{\rm rel}<\infty .
]

Dimensionless gravitational couplings include

[
\tilde G(k)=k^2G(k),
\qquad
\tilde\Lambda(k)=\frac{\Lambda(k)}{k^2},
\qquad
\tilde m_R(k)=\frac{m_R(k)}{k}.
]

This is the UV rule: irrelevant higher-curvature and higher-dimensional matter operators are not freely fit; they are fixed by the RG trajectory once the finite relevant physical parameters are specified.

The spectral-action boundary condition is:

[
S_{\rm bos}(M_s)
================

{\rm Tr},
f\left(
\frac{D_A^2}{M_s^2}
\right)
\simeq
\sum_{n\ge0}
f_{4-n}M_s^{4-n}a_n(D_A^2),

where (D_A) is the fluctuated Dirac operator and (a_n) are heat-kernel coefficients. This is the geometric-unification layer: the SM gauge/Higgs/gravity structure is encoded in spectral data rather than manually glued together.

## 5. Cosmology predicted by MSA-U

For a spatially flat FLRW universe,

[
ds^2=-dt^2+a(t)^2d\vec x^2,
]

the late-time Friedmann equation is approximately

[
H^2
===

\frac{1}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\left[
\rho_r+\rho_b+\rho_c+\rho_\nu+\rho_a+\rho_\phi
\right]
+
\Delta H^2_{R^2}.
]

The scalar dark energy has

[
\rho_\phi=\frac12\dot\phi^2+V_\phi,
\qquad
p_\phi=\frac12\dot\phi^2-V_\phi,

[
w_\phi
======

\frac{p_\phi}{\rho_\phi}.

The axion evolves as

[
\ddot a+3H\dot a+m_a^2(T)f_a\sin(a/f_a)=0.

For (m_a\gg H), the axion behaves like cold dark matter:

[
\rho_a\propto a^{-3}.

For gravitational waves at late times, because the scalar (\phi) is minimally coupled and there is no low-energy Lorentz-violating term,

[
\ddot h_{ij}
+
3H\dot h_{ij}
+
\frac{k^2}{a^2}h_{ij}
=====================

0,
]

so

[
c_T=c.
]

This is important because multimessenger constraints strongly punish models with late-time gravitational-wave speeds different from light speed.

# Why I think MSA-U is the best testable path

MSA-U is not the most mathematically radical theory. String theory, loop quantum gravity, and causal sets are more ambitious. But MSA-U is the **most public-data-testable** synthesis I would bet on because:

1. It exactly nests the best current model.
2. It uses named physical parameters, not opaque fit coefficients.
3. It has direct handles in CMB, BAO, SNe, lensing, galaxy rotation, collider, neutrino, axion, Solar System, GW, and black-hole data.
4. It can absorb the DESI-like “maybe evolving dark energy” signal through one physical slope (\lambda_\phi), while returning to ΛCDM if the signal disappears.
5. It keeps (c_T=c), respects the equivalence principle by construction, and does not require Lorentz violation.
6. It gives a UV-completion criterion through asymptotic safety rather than leaving infinite EFT coefficients arbitrary.

Its biggest gaps are also clear: the asymptotic-safety/spectral boundary is not rigorously solved for the full SM+gravity+axion+(\phi) system; the model still inherits SM flavor parameters; axions and quintessence are not confirmed; and the UV fixed-point trajectory must be computed, not assumed.

# Error metric for comparing all contenders

Use the current baseline

[
T_0={\rm GR+SM+\Lambda CDM+\nu}
]

as the control model.

For a candidate theory (T) with physical parameters (\theta_T), define the joint likelihood:

[
\ln\mathcal L_{\rm joint}(T,\theta_T)
=====================================

\sum_i
\ln\mathcal L_i(D_i\mid T,\theta_T),
]

where (D_i) runs over collider, constants, CMB, BAO, SNe, lensing, Solar System, GW, EHT, pulsar, neutrino, and high-energy messenger data.

For Gaussian data products,

[
\chi_i^2
========

\left[d_i-m_i(T,\theta_T)\right]^T
C_i^{-1}
\left[d_i-m_i(T,\theta_T)\right].
]

Then

[
-2\ln\mathcal L_{\rm joint}
===========================

\sum_i \chi_i^2
]

when Gaussian approximations are valid.

But the real score should punish parameter bloat. I would use Bayesian evidence as the primary metric:

[
Z_T
===

\int d\theta_T,
p(\theta_T\mid T)
\mathcal L_{\rm joint}(T,\theta_T).
]

Compare theories by

[
\Delta\ln Z
===========

\ln Z_T-\ln Z_{T_0}.
]

A simpler engineering score is:

[
\mathcal E(T)
=============

-2\ln\mathcal L_{\rm joint}^{\rm max}
+
2N_{\rm eff}
+
P_{\rm hard}
+
P_{\rm gray}.
]

Where:

[
P_{\rm hard}=
\begin{cases}
0, & \text{if anomaly cancellation, unitarity, causality, Lorentz tests, and GR/SM limits pass},\
+\infty, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
]

And because you do not want gray-box parameters:

[
P_{\rm gray}
============

+\infty
\quad
\text{if any fitted parameter lacks a named physical meaning, dimension, and measurement procedure.}
]

For public-data prediction rather than mere fitting, use train/holdout splits by time:

[
\mathcal E_{\rm pred}(T)
========================

\sum_i
\frac{
-2\ln p(D_i^{\rm holdout}\mid D_i^{\rm train},T)
}{
N_i
}.
]

A theory wins only if it improves held-out prediction after parameter penalties.

\lambda_\phi=0
]

wins, the model collapses to ΛCDM. If (\lambda_\phi\neq0) wins on held-out data, MSA-U beats ΛCDM with one physically interpretable parameter.

### Layer 4: axion dark matter

Fit

[
f_a,\quad m_a,\quad \theta_i
]

to:

* CMB matter density,
* structure growth,
* galaxy rotation/lensing,
* black-hole superradiance constraints,
* laboratory axion searches where public.

If no axion window survives, MSA-U must either drop axion dark matter or lose.

### Layer 5: quantum-gravity consistency

Use asymptotic-safety RG truncations and spectral-action boundary conditions to reduce free UV parameters. This is the hardest layer and where the model is most speculative.

# My ranking of likely winners

If forced to bet today:

1. **Empirical winner for the next decade:** GR + SM + ΛCDM with massive neutrinos, perhaps with a mild dark-energy extension.
2. **Best mathematical unifier:** string/M-theory/holography.
3. **Best QFT-style quantum-gravity program:** asymptotic safety.
4. **Best background-independent geometry program:** loop/spinfoam/GFT.
5. **Best directly testable near-term extensions:** scalar-tensor/dark-energy/modified-gravity EFTs, though they are not full unifications.
6. **Best conservative “unified but testable” synthesis:** the MSA-U model above.

The reason I do not propose a wildly new equation as the winner is simple: public data already constrain deviations from GR+SM incredibly hard. The most powerful theory is likely to look boring at accessible energies and radical only near the Planck scale, black-hole interiors, or the earliest universe. MSA-U is built around that fact.
