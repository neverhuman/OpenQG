# 1. What the brief is actually asking for

The brief is not asking for a grand speculative essay. It is asking for a **derivation-first candidate** that can survive an adversarial benchmark harness.

The core contract is:

1. **No fixture leakage.**
   The candidate cannot copy CODATA, PDG, Planck, DESI, Pantheon+, NuFIT, GWOSC, EHT, SPARC, NIST ASD, or similar reference values and call them predictions.

2. **No hidden calibration.**
   A free parameter fitted to the target fixture is disallowed even if disguised as a “renormalization condition,” “bootstrap value,” “source-state estimate,” or “emergent constant.”

3. **Emit only rows with derivations.**
   If a row is not derived from fixture-independent principles, mark it `not_implemented` or `invalid_domain`.

4. **Use a constants ledger.**
   The candidate must separate:

   * metrology anchors,
   * dimensionless targets,
   * structural integers,
   * inferred state variables,
   * genuine predictions,
   * unsupported quantities.

5. **Prioritize exact structural/null predictions.**
   The safest currently valid predictions are things like:

   * GR deviation parameters equal zero,
   * WEP violation equal zero,
   * Lorentz/CPT violation coefficients equal zero,
     provided the row semantics match those quantities.

6. **Do not confuse law with state.**
   EHT image diameter, GW event masses, SPARC galaxy curves, JPL ephemerides, Pantheon+ supernova distances, and NIST atomic spectra require source-specific or environment-specific state data. A universal law does not determine those rows without independent state inputs.

That is why the best candidates in the archive converge on the same strategy: **derive structure; predict exact nulls; refuse catalog-dependent observables.**

# 3. Shared strengths across the archive

The submissions are strongest when they do four things.

First, they use **almost-commutative spectral geometry / spectral action** as the structural engine for the Standard Model plus gravity. This is a real, source-backed framework: Chamseddine and Connes’ spectral action program showed how the Standard Model action coupled to gravity can arise from the spectrum of a Dirac operator on a noncommutative space. ([arXiv][1])

Second, several tips use the **Pati-Salam / spectral noncommutative geometry bridge** correctly. Chamseddine, Connes, and van Suijlekom showed that relaxing the first-order condition in the noncommutative-geometric construction leads naturally to a Pati-Salam-type structure with (SU(2)_R \times SU(2)_L \times SU(4)). ([arXiv][2])

Third, the best candidates use **anomaly cancellation and Yukawa/Majorana compatibility** to derive hypercharge assignments rather than treating Standard Model charges as arbitrary.

Fourth, the strongest candidates understand the benchmark: they do not try to “predict” galaxy rotation curves, EHT ring diameters, GW source parameters, or atomic line lists without independent source-state data. That is the right move. EHT’s M87 result, for example, is a measurement of a particular astrophysical source with a particular mass, distance, spin/orientation uncertainty, and plasma state; the ring diameter was about (42 \pm 3) microarcseconds, not a universal constant. ([arXiv][3])

# 4. Shared weaknesses and missing pieces

The archive’s biggest weakness is that most candidates give themselves **dossier scores above 85** while the actual quantitative theory-lane coverage remains close to the baseline. The brief itself says the current baseline covers about 12 of 64 families and 96 of 512 rows, i.e. **18.75 theory score**, while strict `openqg_score` remains **0.000**. The stronger candidates improve auditability, not immediate benchmark coverage.

The missing pieces are substantial:

1. **No derived fine-structure constant.**
   The NIST CODATA fine-structure constant is a high-precision dimensionless target, but none of the candidates derive it from first principles. ([NIST][4])

2. **No derived Yukawa spectrum.**
   Electron, muon, tau, quark masses, CKM angles, and PMNS angles remain inputs or unsupported.

3. **No derived QCD scale.**
   (\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}), hadron masses, nuclear binding, and atomic spectra are not computed from a unique UV theory. NIST ASD rows are especially dangerous because atomic spectra require detailed many-body atomic calculations and current conversion constants. ([NIST][5])

4. **No completed cosmology.**
   Planck, DESI, Pantheon+, and NuFIT datasets constrain cosmological and neutrino parameters, but the candidates do not derive a unique cosmological state, dark sector, reheating history, or neutrino mass matrix. Planck 2018 gives a precise (\Lambda)CDM parameter fit; DESI provides BAO constraints; Pantheon+ contains 1701 Type Ia supernova light curves; NuFIT 6.0 updates global neutrino oscillation fits, but these are not derivations from the proposed theories. ([arXiv][6])

5. **No dark matter microphysics.**
   The candidates sometimes mention axions, sterile neutrinos, or geometric dark sectors, but none derives a relic abundance and scattering/cosmology profile.

6. **Generation count is not truly solved.**
   Several tips propose an index relation like (N_{\rm gen}=3). That is attractive, but in the current form it is a structural postulate, not a uniquely derived theorem.

7. **No actual adapter execution.**
   The archive is mostly dossier-level. None of the tips shows a full manifest, adapter, row-level output, and successful `rtk` run.

This is a good OpenQG candidate because it makes a small number of hard, exact claims and refuses everything else.

It is **not** a finished theory of all constants.

# 8. What IASCG-2 is missing

The most important missing derivations are:

1. **A real generation-count theorem.**
   The index idea must become a theorem with uniqueness and stability conditions.

2. **A coupling-constant computation.**
   Need a non-leaky derivation of (\alpha), (\alpha_s), weak mixing angle, and renormalization trajectory.

3. **A flavor theory.**
   Need masses and mixings, not just allowed Yukawa matrices.

4. **A QCD/nuclear bridge.**
   Need (\Lambda_{\rm QCD}), hadron masses, nuclear binding, and low-energy constants.

5. **A dark-sector derivation.**
   Need either axion, sterile neutrino, geometric dark matter, or another mechanism with relic abundance.

6. **A vacuum-energy solution.**
   Must explain why observed (\rho_\Lambda) is tiny without copying cosmological fits.

7. **A cosmological state generator.**
   Need inflation/reheating or alternative initial-condition theory that predicts (A_s,n_s,r,\Omega_i,H_0) without fixture tuning.

8. **A source-state interface.**
   For EHT, GWOSC, SPARC, JPL, and NIST ASD, the theory needs a separate, independently sourced state pack. Without that, it must refuse.
