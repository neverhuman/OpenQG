## Why I chose NCG over string theory

String/M-theory is deeper as a quantum-gravity candidate, but it is much less row-ready under this scoring law because compactification, moduli stabilization, vacuum choice, supersymmetry breaking, Standard Model embedding, and low-energy constants are not uniquely fixed. Douglas’s landscape review explicitly describes string/M theory as having many vacuum configurations that could describe our universe, and says one must compactify extra dimensions to reproduce low-energy physics; this is exactly the kind of unresolved state/choice data that OpenQG punishes. ([INSPIRE][1])

The classic Calabi–Yau heterotic construction is historically important, but the cited (E_8\times E_8) example led to an (E_6) gauge group with four standard generations, not the observed Standard Model as a unique low-energy row engine. ([Princeton University][2])

NCG wins under this benchmark because it is a published geometric mechanism that directly reconstructs a Standard-Model-like gauge/Higgs/fermion structure coupled to gravity from spectral data. The original spectral action paper states that applying the action to the noncommutative space defined by the Standard Model gives the Standard Model action coupled to Einstein plus Weyl gravity and gives high-scale relations among gauge and Higgs couplings. ([arXiv][3])

The 2006–2007 Connes–Chamseddine–Marcolli version includes neutrino mixing, a finite noncommutative space of KO-dimension 6, and a see-saw structure; it is therefore more directly aligned with the OpenQG fixture families than most other human unification programs. ([arXiv][4])

The row-ready Standard Model version uses:

## 2. Formal action

The known NCG action is the spectral action plus fermionic action:

[
S_{\rm NCG}
===========

\operatorname{Tr}\left(f\left(\frac{D_A}{\Lambda}\right)\right)
+
\langle J\psi,D_A\psi\rangle,
]

where (D_A) is the fluctuated Dirac operator. The spectral action is computed by heat-kernel expansion.

For the almost-commutative Standard Model geometry (M\times F_{\rm SM}), van Suijlekom gives:

[
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(
f\left(\frac{D_\omega}{\Lambda}\right)
\right)
\sim
\int_M
\mathcal L(g_{\mu\nu},\Lambda_\mu,Q_\mu,V_\mu,H)
\sqrt g,d^4x
+
O(\Lambda^{-1}),
]

and identifies this as the bosonic Standard Model Lagrangian plus gravitational terms. ([Springer][5])

After unimodularity, the gauge group reduces to the Standard Model gauge group modulo its finite center:

[
\mathfrak G_{\rm SM}
====================

U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)/\mu_6.
]

The same derivation yields the Standard Model hypercharge pattern in the representation of the gauge field on fermions. ([Springer][5])

# 7. Falsifiers and failure modes

NCG-SSM v1 would be damaged by a fundamental Lorentz/CPT violation, a fundamental WEP violation in its declared metric-coupling regime, or a confirmed low-energy particle/gauge structure incompatible with the finite spectral triple’s Standard Model representation.

It would also fail OpenQG review if it emitted (\alpha), Higgs mass, Yukawas, CKM, PMNS, QCD scale, cosmological parameters, EHT angular sizes, GW event masses, or SPARC velocities as predictions. Those are not derived by the published theory without empirical constants, boundary conditions, or source states.
