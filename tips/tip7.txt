## Working conclusion

The strongest path is **not** a gray-box model. The most defensible research program is an **action-based, symmetry-based, EFT-compatible framework** that reduces exactly to the Standard Model, general relativity, and ΛCDM where those are already tested, while adding only physically named fields, masses, couplings, vacuum scales, symmetry-breaking scales, and renormalization-group quantities.

No currently accepted theory is a complete experimentally verified unification of quantum mechanics, gravity, the Standard Model, dark matter, and dark energy. The active frontier is a cluster of programs: string/M-theory and holography, asymptotic safety, loop/spinfoam/group-field quantum gravity, causal dynamical triangulations, causal sets, noncommutative/spectral geometry, GUTs, and conservative effective field theory. This matches the current landscape described by quantum-gravity reviews and institutional overviews: quantum gravity remains the problem of reconciling GR with quantum theory; many approaches exist; and robust unique experimental predictions remain scarce. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy][1])

I would prioritize **public-data falsification** over aesthetic unification. My best proposed model below is therefore a **Causal–Spectral Asymptotic Unification** framework: spectral geometry for matter, SO(10)-like gauge/matter unification, asymptotically safe gravity, scalaron inflation, QCD axion dark matter, and a physically named dark-energy scalar. It is not a proven final theory. It is a complete candidate action plus quantization/renormalization prescription that can be scored against public data.

## 2. The recurring gaps

The same failure modes appear across almost all contenders:

1. **No unique low-energy Standard Model derivation.** Many theories can produce something SM-like, but not one unavoidable SM with the observed masses, mixings, couplings, and vacuum structure.

2. **Dark matter and dark energy are still external.** ΛCDM fits enormous amounts of data, but the microscopic identity of dark matter and the physical nature of dark energy remain unresolved. DESI’s 2025 DR2 BAO results sharpened interest in dynamical dark energy, while DES six-year supernova analyses remain broadly consistent with the standard model, so any new model must handle both directions of evidence. ([DESI][12])

3. **Planck-scale data are indirect.** We mostly test quantum gravity through consistency, low-energy EFT, black holes, cosmology, gravitational waves, Lorentz invariance, and equivalence-principle bounds.

4. **Parameter freedom is dangerous.** A theory with 80 tunable thresholds, brane choices, compactification moduli, or phenomenological functions can fit data but not explain it. The score must penalize nonphysical freedom.

5. **Semiclassical recovery is mandatory.** Any theory must reproduce GR, the SM, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, black-hole mechanics, ΛCDM-like cosmology, and local precision tests in the appropriate limits.

## 6. Error metric for comparing CSAU against contenders

For each model (M), define physical parameters (\theta_M). For every dataset (D_j), compute a forward prediction:

[
\mu_j(\theta_M)=\text{prediction of model }M.
]

For Gaussian summary data:

[
\chi_j^2
========

(y_j-\mu_j)^T C_j^{-1}(y_j-\mu_j).
]

For event-count data such as collider bins or direct-detection bins:

[
-2\ln L_j
=========

2\sum_i
\left[
\mu_i-n_i+n_i\ln\left(\frac{n_i}{\mu_i}\right)
\right].
]

For one-sided limits:

[
-2\ln L_{\rm limit}
===================

\begin{cases}
0, & q(\theta_M)\le q_{\rm lim},[4pt]
\left(\dfrac{q(\theta_M)-q_{\rm lim}}{\sigma_{\rm lim}}\right)^2, & q(\theta_M)>q_{\rm lim}.
\end{cases}
]

Total likelihood score:

[
-2\ln L_{\rm tot}
=================

\sum_j -2\ln L_j.
]

Complexity-penalized score:

[
{\rm BIC}(M)
============

-2\ln L_{\rm max}
+
N_{\rm phys}\ln N_{\rm data},
]

or, for smaller effective samples,

[
{\rm AIC_c}(M)
==============

-2\ln L_{\rm max}
+
2N_{\rm phys}
+
\frac{2N_{\rm phys}(N_{\rm phys}+1)}
{N_{\rm data}-N_{\rm phys}-1}.
]

Interpretability penalty:

[
P_{\rm gray}
============

\lambda_{\rm gray}
\sum_p
\mathbf 1
\left[
p\text{ has no physical unit, symmetry role, field, scale, or boundary condition}
\right].
]

Final score:

[
\boxed{
{\rm Score}(M)
==============

-2\ln L_{\rm test}
+
2N_{\rm eff}
+
P_{\rm gray}
+
P_{\rm violation}.
}
]

Here (P_{\rm violation}=\infty) for hard failures: gauge anomaly, nonunitarity, vacuum instability, wrong low-energy limit, superluminal gravitational waves, violated equivalence-principle bounds, or contradicted particle limits.

The honest way to run this is **time-split validation**:

[
D_{\rm train}
=============

\text{older public releases},
\qquad
D_{\rm test}
============

\text{newer public releases}.
]

For example: fit to Planck/BOSS/Pantheon+/DES Y3/O3-era GW data, then test on DESI DR2, ACT DR6, DES Y6, newer GWOSC releases, Euclid Q1/next releases, Gaia DR4, latest LZ/XENON/PandaX, and new Super-K proton-decay releases.

## 8. How CSAU would be tested first

The first practical scoring pass should not try to solve every sector simultaneously. It should run staged falsification:

### Stage A: consistency gates

Reject immediately if any fail:

[
\text{gauge anomalies}=0,
]

[
V_{\rm GUT}\text{ has stable SM-like vacuum},
]

[
\text{no excluded proton decay},
]

[
c_{\rm GW}=c,
]

[
\eta_{\rm EP}\lesssim 10^{-15},
]

[
\text{unitarity/stability of higher-curvature sector}.
]

### Stage B: low-energy calibration

Fit only named parameters to:

[
{m_f,;V_{\rm CKM},;U_{\rm PMNS},;g_1,g_2,g_3,;m_H,;\lambda_H,;\Omega_b,;\Omega_{\rm DM},;A_s,n_s,H_0,\sigma_8}.
]

### Stage C: cross-validated public test

Hold out:

[
D_{\rm holdout}
===============

{\text{DESI DR2},\text{ACT DR6},\text{DES Y6},\text{new GWOSC},\text{Euclid Q1/next},\text{new Super-K},\text{new LZ/XENON/PandaX}}.
]

Compare:

[
\Delta{\rm Score}
=================

## {\rm Score}({\rm CSAU})

{\rm Score}({\rm SM+GR+\Lambda CDM+EFT}).
]

CSAU only wins if:

[
\Delta{\rm Score}<0
]

after complexity and gray-box penalties.

## 9. My honest bet

The model most likely to survive public testing is **not** a radical replacement of everything. It is a conservative unification stack:

[
\boxed{
\text{SM + GR + EFT}
\quad\subset\quad
\text{spectral SO(10) matter}
\quad+\quad
\text{asymptotically safe gravity}
\quad+\quad
R^2\text{ inflation}
\quad+\quad
\text{QCD axion}
\quad+\quad
\text{minimal dynamical dark energy}.
}
]

Why this is the best bet:

* It keeps every spectacular success of SM, GR, and ΛCDM.
* It avoids anonymous parameters.
* It has real quantum-gravity ambition through asymptotic safety.
* It has real matter unification through spectral/SO(10)-style structure.
* It explains neutrino masses, strong CP, inflation, and dark matter with named mechanisms.
* It is falsifiable by public data: proton decay, neutrino data, CMB tensors, DESI/DES/SNe, axion bounds, equivalence-principle tests, GW propagation, and collider limits.

Its biggest weaknesses are also clear:

[
\boxed{
\text{AS fixed point for this exact matter content may not exist;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{SO(10) thresholds may require too much tuning;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{the Weyl-squared sector must avoid physical ghosts;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{dark energy scalar may be unnecessary if }\Lambda\text{ wins;}
}
]

[
\boxed{
\text{axion parameter space may shrink under future data.}
}
]

So the right research posture is: **treat CSAU as a complete candidate to score, not as a declared final truth.** Its merit is that it is mathematically explicit, physically interpretable, and testable against the largest set of public physics data we have.
