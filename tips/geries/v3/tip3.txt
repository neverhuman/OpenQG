Fourth, the strongest tips understand that catalog observables are not automatically law observables. # 4. Where the archive is weak

The dominant weakness is **score inflation**.

Many tips effectively say:

> We wrote equations for every domain, marked hard rows unsupported, and therefore deserve 86–88.

That is not strict enough. Writing a governing equation is not the same as deriving a fixture-ready number.

Examples:

| Written equation                  | Why it is not enough                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| (V_{\rm CKM}=U_u^\dagger U_d)     | This defines CKM after Yukawas are known; it does not derive CKM angles.                                     |
| (m_f=y_f v/\sqrt{2})              | This maps Yukawas to masses; it does not derive Yukawas or (v).                                              |
| Friedmann equations               | They do not derive Planck/DESI/BOSS/Pantheon rows without a derived cosmological state.                      |
| Kerr shadow formula               | It does not derive EHT rows without independent mass, distance, spin, inclination, and emission assumptions. |
| QCD beta function                 | It does not derive hadron masses or atomic/nuclear corrections.                                              |
| RAR/MOND-like galaxy equation     | It does not derive SPARC rows without baryonic profiles, mass-to-light ratios, and radius/state data.        |
| Fixed-point condition (\beta_i=0) | It is a program, not a number, unless the fixed point is solved and uncertainty-propagated.                  |

The second weakness is that many “derived” structures are actually **selected inputs**. The finite algebra

[
\mathcal A_F=\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb H\oplus M_3(\mathbb C)
]

does a lot of useful work once chosen, but most candidates do not derive why this algebra, why this finite Hilbert space, why three generations, or why the observed Yukawa texture.

The third weakness is provenance. The brief wants named source-pack or admitted local provenance. Several tips cite general literature or add new mechanisms—“spectral torsion,” “trace-anomaly bootstrap,” “orbifold selector,” “spectral entropy,” “generation index”—without proving them or integrating them through the source-pack process.

The fourth weakness is executable evidence. The archive contains writeups, not a run against the actual OpenQG fixture tree. No tip actually proves row-shape compatibility by running `rtk just fast`, `rtk just universal-micro`, or `rtk just universal-mini`.

The fifth weakness is the treatment of conditional rows. Conditional formulas are valuable, but they must not count unless the row metadata supplies independent state variables. Otherwise they are a leakage/tuning trap.
