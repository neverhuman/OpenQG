# 7. What the archive gets right

The archive is strong in four ways.

First, it understands the brief’s anti-cheating rule: **unsupported rows are acceptable; dishonest numeric rows are fatal.**

Second, it correctly separates **law rows** from **state rows**. A theory may derive GR, but it does not thereby derive the mass of a particular GWOSC binary. It may derive Kerr geometry, but not an EHT angular diameter without independent mass, distance, spin, inclination, and emission-state inputs. It may derive orbital dynamics, but not JPL Horizons rows without initial conditions.

Third, it identifies the right immediately safe row families: GR null tests, WEP null tests, and Lorentz/CPT null coefficients.

Fourth, it has a plausible structural foundation: the almost-commutative spectral algebra can support the SM gauge skeleton, color, chirality, charge quantization, and anomaly cancellation.
